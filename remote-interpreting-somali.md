# What Remote Interpreting Is and Why It Matters for Somali-Speaking Communities

*By Zakaria Arap, Somali · Swahili · English Interpreter & Translator*

---

A mother sits in a hospital waiting room in a city far from home. Her son has had a fever for three days. When the doctor finally calls them in, she knows exactly what she wants to say: when the fever started, what she has already tried, and what worries her most. But the words she knows are in Somali, and the doctor speaks only English.

Not long ago, she might have relied on a relative, a neighbour, or even her own child to translate. Today, the doctor can pick up a phone or open a video call, and within minutes a trained Somali interpreter joins the conversation.

That is **remote interpreting**, and for Somali-speaking communities around the world, it is quietly changing how people access healthcare, justice, education and public services.

## What Is Remote Interpreting?

Remote interpreting is real-time spoken interpretation delivered over a phone or video connection instead of in person. It typically takes two forms:

| Type | How it works | Best used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Over-the-Phone Interpreting (OPI)** | The interpreter joins by audio only | Short, urgent or routine conversations |
| **Video Remote Interpreting (VRI)** | The interpreter appears on screen | Situations where facial expressions, gestures or visual context matter |

The process is straightforward:

1. A service provider (a clinic, school, court or government office) connects to an interpreting service.
2. An interpreter fluent in both languages joins the call.
3. The interpreter conveys what each party says, accurately and in real time, until the conversation ends.

### Interpreting vs. Translation

The two terms are often used interchangeably, but they describe different skills:

- **Translation** deals with *written* content such as documents, websites and forms. The translator has time to research and revise.
- **Interpreting** deals with *spoken* language in real time. The interpreter must listen, understand and render meaning accurately within seconds.

## Why Somali Speakers Need It

Somali-speaking communities are spread across many countries, from Kenya, Somalia and Ethiopia to the United Kingdom, the United States and the Nordic region. In many of these places, older community members and recent arrivals may not yet speak the local language with confidence.

That language gap matters most in high-stakes settings:

- **Healthcare**, where a misunderstood symptom, allergy or dosage can directly affect someone's health.
- **Legal and immigration matters**, where a single word can shape an outcome.
- **Education**, where parents need to understand their child's progress, needs and rights.
- **Social services**, where people must explain their circumstances clearly to receive support.

Without access to a professional interpreter, families often depend on children or untrained relatives. This can place young people in difficult and inappropriate situations, and it increases the risk of errors, because everyday fluency is not the same as accurate, impartial interpretation.

## Why "Remote" Makes Such a Difference

In-person interpreting still has its place, but remote interpreting solves problems that in-person services often cannot:

- **Speed.** An interpreter can join within minutes rather than being booked days in advance.
- **Reach.** A Somali interpreter based in Nairobi can support a patient in London or Minneapolis in the same hour.
- **Availability.** There are far fewer trained Somali interpreters than there are for widely spoken languages. Remote work allows each interpreter to serve many more people.
- **Cost.** Organizations avoid travel time and minimum booking fees, making it more practical to offer interpreting at all.

For a language with a large, global community but a limited pool of professional interpreters, these advantages are significant.

## What Makes a Good Somali Interpreter

Being bilingual is only the starting point. Professional interpreting also demands:

- **Accuracy:** conveying exactly what was said, without adding, softening or omitting anything.
- **Impartiality:** remaining neutral, even in emotional or tense conversations.
- **Confidentiality:** protecting everything heard during a session.
- **Cultural competence:** understanding how dialects, respectful forms of address and sensitive topics shape how a message is received.
- **Technical reliability:** working from a quiet space with a good headset and a stable internet connection, because a dropped call during a medical consultation is not a minor inconvenience.

## The Road Ahead

As healthcare systems, courts and public services become more digital, remote interpreting will only grow in importance. For Somali-speaking communities, this creates an opportunity: better, faster access to essential services, and a growing need for skilled, professional interpreters who understand both the language and the people who speak it.

## Conclusion

Back in that hospital room, the difference is simple. With an interpreter on the line, the mother explains her son's symptoms in her own words, asks her questions, and leaves understanding the treatment plan. No one has to guess, and no child has to carry an adult's responsibility.

Language should never stand between a person and the help they need. Remote interpreting makes that possible on a global scale, and skilled Somali interpreters are at the heart of it.
